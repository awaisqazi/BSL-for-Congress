# Campaign Voice Guide

How this site, and every future update to it, should sound. Built from Byron's
August 18, 2026 re-election launch speech at Indígó MRKTŌ (transcribed from the
campaign's own recording) and from how the room talked about him that night.
Read this before writing or revising any copy. See `design.md` for the visual side.

## The one rule

**Speak as the campaign, in the campaign's voice, about our own record.**
Never narrate what a newspaper reported. Press and endorsements are receipts we
show, never the subject of the sentence. "More than 800 affordable homes created
or preserved. The Tribune endorsed him for it." Not "The Tribune credits Byron
with..."

## The center of gravity: "we"

Byron's refrain that night, repeated a dozen times: **"It has never been about
me. It has always been about we. We, the people."** That is the spine of the
voice. Byron is the organizer in the story, not its hero. The community is the
protagonist and the campaign is what the neighbors built.

- Lead with what the ward did together, then what Byron's role was.
- "The community organized, fought back, and bought those lots." Not "Byron won
  the lots."
- When Byron is the subject, the verbs are showing up, standing with, fighting
  alongside, answering the phone.
- "Hoy por mí, mañana por ti." Solidarity is a practice with a direction, not a
  slogan. Use it in Spanish on both language versions when it fits.

## How he talks

**Plain, front-porch, and concrete.** Short declarative sentences. Real
addresses, real numbers, real names of places. He says "18th and Peoria," "6.3
acres," "a baby was born in that shelter," "7,000 essential workers died." He
does not say "stakeholders," "equity framework," or "community-centered
solutions." If a sentence would sound fine in a foundation grant, rewrite it.

**Receipts over adjectives.** He proves character with a timeline, not a
description. Do not call him "tireless" or "bold"; say what happened and when.
"In 48 hours, when government looked the other way, this community opened a
shelter for almost 400 people, and a baby was born there instead of in a police
station."

**Bilingual by nature, not by translation.** He moves between English and
Spanish mid-thought. Spanish phrases belong in the English copy where he would
actually say them: "Sí se puede," "Hoy por mí, mañana por ti," "El pueblo unido."
The Spanish version of the site is its own writing, not a translation of the
English; keep the same facts and the same warmth, and let it read like a
neighbor wrote it.

**Warm to the room, hard on the powerful.** Two registers, never blended:

- To neighbors: gratitude, humor about the triplets, "look around this room,"
  "this is home," names of the people who did the work (teachers, Dr. Figueroa,
  the food pantry, the Chinatown neighbors who sent PPE).
- About the machine and billionaires: blunt, unapologetic, specific. "They can
  pay their taxes." "Solidarity with actions, not with propaganda, not with the
  people who come for the selfies when it is convenient." "My predecessor was an
  FBI informant and still walks around like nothing happened." "I make no
  apology for that."

**Moral clarity without a lecture.** He names the stakes as family stakes: "What
father would not do the same thing?" "Who would deny a plate of food to a
child?" Questions like these do the work that a policy paragraph would
otherwise do.

**"When we fight, we win."** Every hard story ends in what the people won, and
the win is always shared. Never end a section on the injury; end on the
organizing.

## What he talks about, in his order

1. **Who is in the room.** Teachers, the food pantry, tenants, small businesses,
   young organizers, the elected school board slate. The campaign is a coalition
   of people who already do the work.
2. **Why he ran in 2015 and why it was never a career.** 20,000 neighbors pushed
   out of Pilsen by neglect, corruption, and silence. A predecessor who wore a
   wire for the FBI. Keeping the schools open.
3. **What the ward did together.** The pandemic (Ward Night never stopped, PPE
   door to door, more than $100,000 raised "not by the billionaires, by the
   people," Chinatown neighbors sending vaccines and PPE). The 48-hour shelter
   for almost 400 new neighbors. A mental health clinic reopened. A new clinic
   with dental care for kids. The largest community air monitoring network in the
   country. 18th and Peoria: 500 luxury units stopped, 6.3 acres bought by the
   city, all-affordable homes on the way.
4. **What is still unfinished.** Tenant protections (the Protect Renters
   Ordinance), housing as a human right, health care for the seniors the state
   cut off, an elected school board that actually governs, an environment
   department that actually inspects.
5. **The ask.** Doors, Tuesdays, February 23. Bring a neighbor.

## Words and moves to avoid

- Third-party narration of our own record ("according to," "X reported,"
  "credits him with").
- Nonprofit and consultant vocabulary: stakeholders, equity lens, leverage,
  impactful, community-centered, holistic.
- Hero framing: "champion," "tireless," "bold leader," "fighter for" as a title.
  He is a teacher and organizer; let the record say the rest.
- Naming opponents in the ward race. The contrast is machine versus movement,
  never a person.
- Presenting goals as caps ("only $X to go"). Goals are floors.
- Emdashes. Use commas, colons, or a new sentence.
- Amplifying fights that are not the ward's (the Pritzker exchange, the
  congressional saga beyond its one chapter on the Movement page).
- Chinatown is a solidarity story, not a ward boundary claim. Do not describe it
  as part of the 25th.

## A quick test before publishing

Read the paragraph aloud in Byron's voice, standing in a packed room in Pilsen
with the triplets somewhere near the front. If it sounds like a press release,
cut the adjectives and add an address. If Byron is the hero of the sentence,
give the sentence back to the neighbors.
